Re intuition for *when* priors will be discrete, maybe it’s helpful to know when they won’t be.

Take p(x|θ) ∝ e^(x-θ)^2/2 gaussian σ=1. If θ lives in some finite interval of the real line, then I think p*(θ) is always discrete.

One way to make the prior continuous is to make the interval infinitely long. We have this scaling law which says the number of points of weight grows slightly faster than L/σ, which means the average spacing between them shrinks (relative to σ) thus approaching a continuum.

The number of distinguishable parameter values is about L, so the information to encode which one is about log(L), so infinite L means infinite information. That never happens in practice, any real measurement can be written down in only so many digits.

Another way to make the prior continuous is to introduce a perfect symmetry. For instance if θ and x here both live on a circle, [0,L] with the ends identified, then I think the prior will respect this.

Re intuition for *why* the prior is discrete… I have better intuition from the equivalent minimax formulation (e.g. footnote on 3rd page of the PNAS paper). I attach some slides from a talk in which I tried to explain this. This is for the Bernoulli model, eq [1] in the paper.

When θ₀ is near to the boundary, then p(x|θ₀) is more sharply peaked. So for flat p(x), D_KL() is larger there, so your opponent will choose θ₀ at the boundary, and you will lose a lot. You can improve things by moving more weight in p(θ) towards the boundary, from nearby, and are incentivised to keep doing so until eventually you end up with a delta function there, and zero weight nearby. Note that p(x) does not develop any gaps.

This problem of maximising I(X;Θ) over the prior is the simplest of various problems in which you maximise some information. I think all the information bottleneck ideas are not very far away, but they are a bit more complicated as they have three things X,Θ,Y. I believe that many of them also tend to lead to discrete solutions, under similar conditions (enough noise that all informations are finite, and no exact symmetries) although I suppose that’s a wooly statement.
